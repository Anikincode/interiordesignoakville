The Interior Design Process, Step by Step

Every project passes through the same disciplined sequence, from the first measurement to the final report.

In short

The firm's process has seven stages: space planning; concept and material selection; client approval with a drawing package; permits and approvals; tender and project coordination; final decisions and walk-through; and a completion report. Each stage depends on the one before it, and changes are cheapest in the early stages.

Below: each stage in order and what to expect from it.

1. Space planning

The space is measured, mapped and evaluated for how it is actually used. Traffic flow, sightlines and constraints are established, setting the limits for every later decision.

2. Concept and material selection

Mood, material and function are brought into one direction, with finishes, textures, lighting and millwork evaluated against durability, budget and the client's own sensibility.

3. Client approval with a drawing package

Floor plans, elevations, millwork details, lighting layouts and specifications are reviewed in detail. Revisions are made here, where they are inexpensive.

4. Permits and approvals

Documentation is prepared and submitted, and any conditions attached to a permit are resolved before construction begins.

5. Tender and project coordination

Bids from qualified trades are compared on capability and fit as well as price. Trades are scheduled and sequenced, and site questions are resolved as they arise.

6. Final decisions and walk-through

Remaining choices, such as furnishing placement and final fixtures, are confirmed. A room by room walk-through measures the result against the approved drawings.

7. Completion report

A documented record of the finished space, the decisions made and the warranty and maintenance information for the materials used.

The work is led by Lora Kiriboy, founder of D'Lora Luxe Design Inc. Her training in architecture and international project experience inform every plan, drawing and site review.
