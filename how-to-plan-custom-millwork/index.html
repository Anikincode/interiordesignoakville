How to Plan Custom Millwork for a Home

Custom millwork goes wrong when it is ordered before it is understood.

In short

Plan custom millwork in five steps: define what the piece must store or display, measure the real space, choose materials and hardware, coordinate lighting and services, and approve detailed drawings before fabrication. Skipping any step usually shows up later as a gap, a clash or a change order.

Below: the five steps in order, with the mistake each one prevents.

1. What must the piece do?

List what it will hold or display, who will use it and how often. A pantry, a media wall and a wardrobe need different depths, shelf spacing and hardware.

2. Measure the actual space

Walls are rarely square. Measure at several heights and note baseboards, vents and switches, so the piece is drawn to the room that exists.

3. Choose materials and hardware

Select finishes and hardware together. Door style, hinge type and edge detail affect cost, appearance and how well the unit lasts.

4. Coordinate lighting and services

Locate integrated lights, outlets and ventilation on the drawings so nothing is cut into finished cabinetry afterwards.

5. Approve detailed drawings

Review plans, elevations and sections with dimensions before any fabrication begins. This is the last point at which changes are simple.
